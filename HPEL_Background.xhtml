Background

Reddit, the self proclaimed “heart of the internet,” has over 100,000 active communities, or subreddits, which contain, as a collective, over 24 billion posts and comments. The website was initially proposed to function as a “front page of the internet,” a place where users could more easily sift through news and other available information, and was founded by Steve Huffman and Alexis Ohanian in June of 2005. The ability for users to post any type of content was central to the initial design of the website, as was the voting system, composed of upvotes and downvotes, to determine the content that was displayed most prominently. The concept of “karma,” which is earned when other users upvote a post, was an important source of credibility on the original website, and it retains this role today, despite the changes that have been made, the most important of which was the introduction of the ability to comment on posts.

Both Ohanian and Huffman left the company in 2009, but returned amidst controversy regarding content and censorship in 2014 and 2015, as executive chair and CEO respectively. The founders helped to oversee efforts regarding stricter moderation policies in response to Reddit’s growing reputation for “offensive content and toxic behavior” (Caffrey), which grew out of a policy subjecting users only to basic restrictions in an attempt to balance user freedom and suppression of dangerous or offensive content. These policies have since been updated, in part due to Huffman leading the process of banning subreddits known to be racist or exploitative in 2015, and a redesigned version of the website was released in April of 2018.

The balance between censorship and safety has been a precarious one, and Reddit has been criticized for perceived suppression of right-wing viewpoints, as well as allowing the spread of misinformation, particularly in regard to the COVID-19 pandemic. These criticisms, as well as the reasoning behind them, could lead to some users attempting to police their own language in order to ensure that it is seen, particularly because of the influence that the voting system has over the content that is promoted or suppressed. Additionally, because the English language “has been at the center of the development of digital technology” (Guiang, 5), it is perceived as “the Lingua Franca of the Internet even if there are other languages that are being used online” (Guiang, 5). Reddit is not exempt from this phenomenon, and users on many geographically-specific subreddits, such as r/Philippines, on which, “although most of the Redditors are Filipinos, it can be observed that most of them prefer to use the English Language” (Guiang, 2).
